Instructions

Press the SPACEBAR (or click the on-screen button) ONLY when the exact same shape AND color appear twice in a row. Do not press for non-matches.

  • One shape flashes every 2 seconds (shown for 0.2 s, then a blank screen).
  • Sit so your head and shoulders fill the dashed box, about arm's length from the screen.
  • The test opens with a few seconds of calibration — sit still while it measures your camera's baseline noise.
  • Press Esc or the Stop button to end early; you still get a report for what you completed.

How this compares with a clinical QbTest

  • Same task design as publicly described for the adult version: remember-the-previous-one matching on four shape/color stimuli, 200 ms on screen, one every 2 seconds, 600 trials over 20 minutes, 25% targets.
  • No norm group. A clinical report scores you against people of your age and sex. This app has no such data, so it can only compare you with your own earlier sessions. A number here is not high or low in any clinical sense.
  • Different movement measurement. The clinical test follows a reflective marker on your head with an infrared camera. This app estimates head position from the webcam picture with a face-tracking model and converts it to centimetres from the distance between your pupils. It measures the same kind of quantity (distance, area, time active, micro-movements), but with a slower, coarser camera and an assumed pupil distance, so treat the centimetres as approximate.
  • Consumer hardware. Your keyboard and display add a roughly constant delay to every reaction time. Differences between your own sessions on the same computer are meaningful; the absolute milliseconds are not comparable with clinical equipment.

How the numbers are computed

  • Omission: a target (exact repeat) with no press in its 2-second window. Commission: a press on a non-target (lure or different) at 150 ms or later.
  • Anticipatory: any press faster than 150 ms after stimulus onset. These are counted on their own and excluded from hits, commissions and reaction-time statistics.
  • Mean RT / RT variability: mean and sample standard deviation of reaction times for correct hits. Sensitivity d′: how well you separate targets from non-targets, from hit rate and false-press rate (log-linear corrected).
  • Timing check: the run fails if any stimulus was skipped, appeared more than 40 ms late, or stayed on screen outside 170–240 ms. Failed runs are not added to your history.
  • Head movement: about 15 times a second the app finds the point midway between your eyes. Pixels become millimetres using your pupil distance (63 mm unless you enter your own). Distance: total path travelled, counted in steps of at least 1 mm so camera jitter is not added up. Micro-movements: the number of those steps. Time active: share of time the head moved faster than 1 cm per second. Area: the region the head point covered, in 5 mm squares. Only movement across the picture is measured, not towards or away from the camera.
  • Activity score: frame-to-frame luminance change inside the dashed box, minus your calibration baseline, scaled so 1.0 is the movement threshold. Motion index: area under that score (score × seconds). Active time: share of samples at or above the threshold. Bursts: separate runs of above-threshold movement.
  • Lighting changes, camera auto-exposure and people moving behind you all register as movement. Compare sessions only when recorded in the same setup.
